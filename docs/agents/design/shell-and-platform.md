# Shell and platform

Read this before changing the app frame, bottom navigation, continuous corners, safe areas, the PWA touch guards, pull-to-refresh, page entrances, or the landscape phone layout.

## Bottom navigation

### Material

The mobile bottom navigation is a floating Liquid Glass-style capsule, inset from the screen edges and lifted above the home indicator, rather than an edge-attached bar. Safari exposes no native glass or backdrop refraction to web content, so the material is layered by hand: a lightly tinted translucent fill, a light `backdrop-filter` blur with saturation so content stays visible through the bar, a top specular sheen, and a light-catching gradient rim stroked along the continuous outline (a masked CSS rim until that outline is measured), with an opaque fallback where `backdrop-filter` is unsupported. The glass lives on its own `.bottom-nav-surface` layer rather than on the nav: the continuous-corner clip-path makes its element a backdrop root, so on the nav itself it would blank the blur. Write only the unprefixed `backdrop-filter` and `mask`: Tailwind's Lightning CSS generates the `-webkit-` forms, and a hand-written prefixed declaration after the standard one replaces it in the output, leaving Chromium with no blur at all.

### Active-tab lens

A single glass lens pill marks the active tab and slides between tabs on a spring curve with slight overshoot, squash-stretching as it travels. It must not stretch on first render, only on a tab change. Pressing the active tab swells the lens; tab icons never scale on press. The bar's inner width snaps to a multiple of 4 and its left edge to a whole pixel with CSS `round()`, so every tab edge and lens stop lands on a whole pixel; a half-pixel split lets the lens and tab layers round in different directions, visibly off-center on a phone. Tabs are not link-draggable, and the lens and tabs keep permanent compositing layers (`will-change: transform`) so icons do not re-snap by half a pixel while the lens animates. Reduced motion removes the slide and stretch.

### Load and sizing

The bar and lens use continuous corners (below), and the bar waits offscreen until its shape is measured, then slides up on load; only `transform` animates, because opacity below 1 would make the nav a backdrop root and flash the glass clear. `--bottom-nav-height` is the capsule's full footprint including its bottom offset; content padding, scroll padding, the toast offset, and drag edge auto-scroll all derive from it or from the nav's measured top.

### Shadow and outline on iOS

WebKit does not clip a `backdrop-filter` to a clip-path, so on iOS the blur painted the plain circular capsule and showed as a light rim past the smoothed ends; the surface therefore also carries a mask of its continuous outline (`continuousMask`), which WebKit does apply to the blur. That mask would cut off a shadow on the surface itself, so the drop shadow lives on a sibling `.bottom-nav-shadow` layer behind it: a plain `box-shadow` inset 1 px, so the shadow starts under the smoothed edge instead of leaving an unshadowed sliver between the edge and the circular shadow, and opted out of the native superellipse, which squared its ends in Chromium. The outline's hairline is a 6 % `ContinuousStroke` drawn along the curve, not a `0 0 0 0.5px` box-shadow ring, which followed the circle and hid the capsule smoothing entirely. Do not draw that shadow with an SVG blur or a `drop-shadow` filter on a filled copy of the outline: iOS Safari clipped the SVG blur to a rectangle, and the filled copy's antialiased edge leaked a dark line along the ends.

## Continuous corners

### Tokens and native corners

Corners are continuous (curvature-smoothed) with one curve drawn two ways, set by three tokens in `tokens.css`: `--corner-superellipse` (1.8; higher is smoother and squarer), `--corner-extent` (1.55; how far past the authored radius the curve reaches, higher is rounder), and `--capsule-smoothing` (0.3) for pill ends. Apple's own corner is about superellipse 1.38 at extent 1.24, which at this app's sizes is within a fraction of a pixel of a plain circle, so the app deliberately runs more continuous than that. Where the browser draws `corner-shape` natively (Chromium today), one `@supports` rule gives every element the superellipse and sets `--corner-radius-scale` to the extent; every rectangular `border-radius` is therefore authored as `calc(Npx * var(--corner-radius-scale))`, and new ones must be too, while circles (`50%`) and pills (`999px`) stay literal. Elsewhere the scale stays 1 and corners are plain circles.

### Script-drawn corners

On iOS today, continuous corners come from `src/components/ui/continuous-corners/`: `useContinuousCorners` for the tab bar and lens, the Budget and Income summary cards, and every sheet, and `ContinuousControls`, mounted once in `providers.tsx`, which attaches to every element matching its button selector list (primary and soft buttons, the Expense/Income segmented control and its thumb, filter tabs, the icon, tone, and organizer action buttons, the sheet header submit, the month picker control and its month cells, and the toast action) as they enter the DOM. Add a class to that list when a new button style needs continuous corners. Both apply the same superellipse and extent (their elements keep a scale of 1), or a capsule end when the radius fills a side. The element paints the largest circular radius that contains the curve and its clip-path removes only the slivers between that circle and the curve, so shadows, focus rings, and contents keep painting normally. A CSS border stays and is also stroked along the curve as a background layer, re-read whenever a state attribute, hover, or border transition changes it. That layer is `!important`, because component state rules restyle with the `background` shorthand, which would reset it at any specificity. The border color must be opaque so the two do not double up, and a bordered continuous element must use `background-color` rather than a background image.

### Mechanics

The mechanics, all in `src/components/ui/continuous-corners/`: `geometry.ts` first mirrors CSS by shrinking radii that overflow a side together by one factor, then scales each by the extent. A corner whose scaled radius fills a whole side becomes a capsule end, whose curvature ramps linearly from zero (a clothoid) through the `--capsule-smoothing` share of its 90° turn along the longer edge and then holds as a circular arc; every other corner is the `superellipse(K)` curve with exponent 2^K, exactly what `corner-shape` draws. `attach.ts` sizes from the ResizeObserver border-box size, which is fractional and ignores transforms: `offsetWidth` rounds, and the half-pixel error was visible at the tab bar's 4 px gaps. `useContinuousCorners` returns a callback ref, so an element that remounts (the lens replays its stretch by remounting) is re-attached, and `ContinuousControls` skips elements a hook already manages. `ContinuousStroke` draws a gradient rim inside the outline for surfaces that have no CSS border, which is how the tab bar and lens get their light-catching edges.

### Constraints

A decorative shape attached this way must be a real element, not a pseudo-element, which is why the segmented control's thumb is a `<span>`. The clip applies to the element's whole subtree, so anything overhanging a continuous element's corner must be its sibling, not its child: the Transactions filter count badge sits beside the filter button in a positioned wrapper for that reason. A radius scaled past half an element's side becomes a circle or pill end, and one that nearly reaches it leaves almost no straight edge: a small square control reads as a circle. Keep the authored radius times the extent to about 85% of half the element's shorter side, which is why the 46 px filter button, sized to match the search input's height, uses 12 px rather than the input's 14 px. Circles and pills keep `corner-shape: round`: add any new one to the opt-out list in `tokens.css`, or the native superellipse will square it off.

## Status bar and safe area

### Drawing under the status bar

The installed iOS app draws under the status bar: `appleWebApp.statusBarStyle` is `black-translucent` with `viewport-fit=cover`, so the page, including the hold-menu blur and full-screen sheets, reaches the top edge instead of stopping at an opaque system bar. `env(safe-area-inset-top)` is therefore the status bar's height in standalone mode, and every top-anchored surface must add it through the `--safe-area-top` token in `tokens.css`, never through `env(safe-area-inset-top)` directly (launch race below): the mobile header's top padding, the navigation-detail header, full-screen sheets, the keyboard-open chat, the hold-menu layer, Better Buddy's resting clamp, and the sync pill, which sits `calc(var(--safe-area-top) + 12px)` from the top so it stays 12 px into the header. A new fixed or sticky element near the top must do the same, or it will sit under the clock.

### Viewport height

In this mode `height: 100%` on `html` and `body` resolves to the screen minus the status bar, and while the root is that short iOS also sizes the layout viewport to match, so `100dvh`, `100svh`, and a fixed element's `bottom: 0` all stopped a status bar short of the real bottom edge, leaving a white band under the bottom navigation. `tokens.css` therefore defines `--viewport-height` (`100dvh`, or `100lvh` under `@media (display-mode: standalone)`, the one unit that reports the full screen there), and `html`, `body`, the app frame and main column, the sign-in screen, the navigation detail, and full-screen sheets use it. Sizing the root that way is what fixes the layout viewport: measured on device afterwards, `innerHeight` and `100dvh` were the full 874 px from launch and a fixed `bottom: 0` reached the real edge, so bottom-anchored fixed surfaces keep their plain `bottom` offsets. An interim version also shifted every bottom-anchored surface down by `calc(100lvh - 100dvh)`; that was zero at rest but became the status bar's height whenever iOS briefly shrank `100dvh` as the keyboard opened, which animated the Better Buddy chat's bottom edge on its own timer and made the composer and title bounce. Do not base a position on `100lvh - 100dvh`, and do not write `100dvh` on a new full-height element; use `--viewport-height`.

### Late inset on launch

On an occasional cold launch iOS reports `env(safe-area-inset-top)` as 0 for the first frame or two, so the header painted on the clock's row and then dropped about 48 px; a screen recording caught it in production as well as under `next dev`. `--safe-area-top` is `max(env(safe-area-inset-top), var(--safe-area-top-launch, 0px))`, and the inline head script in `src/app/safe-area-launch.ts` (installed app, portrait only) measures a hidden probe sized by `env()` before the first paint. If iOS already reports the inset it only saves it; otherwise it sets `--safe-area-top-launch` on `html` to the height saved in `localStorage` as `betterBudgetSafeAreaTop` on an earlier launch, so the first frame already has the real inset. The first launch after installing has nothing saved (Home Screen apps do not share Safari's storage, and Safari reports a top inset of 0), so there the script instead sets `data-safe-area-pending` on `html`, and `tokens.css` keeps `body` hidden and pauses every CSS animation (`animation-play-state: paused !important`) until it is removed, because the entrance animations (page blur-in, bottom navigation slide, summary arc) would otherwise run while hidden and appear half finished. On that launch a late inset therefore shows as the splash fading to white for a frame or two instead of a jump. A fixed 59 px stand-in, the common Dynamic Island inset, was considered for that launch and not used, because it moves the header whenever the phone's real inset differs. The script then reads the probe every frame. Once iOS reports the inset it reveals the page and removes the launch value, since with a saved value the two are equal and removing it moves nothing, and it saves the real one. It reveals the page after 600 ms regardless and stops polling after 3 s. Landscape launches skip the fallback, since the top inset is 0 there. An earlier version hid the page on every launch until the inset arrived; a slow launch then showed as a white pause each time, so hiding is now limited to the first launch. `html` carries `suppressHydrationWarning` because the script writes its inline style before hydration.

## Touch guards, scroll surface, and month slide

### Touch guards

The mobile PWA intentionally disables page zoom, text selection, touch callouts, document-level pull-to-refresh, and cancelable Safari history gestures beginning within the leftmost 20 px on any route. The global touch guard must remain capture-phase and non-passive for touchstart and touchmove, claim non-control edge touches immediately, preserve normal control taps, and prevent every move that begins inside the edge strip regardless of direction. Mount it once above all routes.

### Field focus on touch

On touch screens (`pointer: coarse`), a focused field shows focus with its own border instead of a ring. The light blue `:focus-visible` outline from `app-shell.css` is removed for text fields, selects, textareas, and the allocation picker's checkboxes. The Income expected-amount field loses its halo too. Bordered fields (`.field` inputs, selects, and textareas, the Transactions search, the Income expected amount, and the date field's `.date-input-shell` through `:focus-within`) turn their border to the theme `--blue` over 0.2 s instead, and back over the same 0.2 s on blur. The expected amount snapped both ways until 5.11.3, because it is not a `.field` input. These rules live in the `pointer: coarse` block near the end of `responsive-motion.css`, because that file comes after every area file and so wins over their more specific rules. The rules use `:focus` rather than `:focus-visible`, because iOS does not always match `:focus-visible` on a tapped select. Buttons keep their rings, and fine-pointer devices keep every ring for keyboard users.

### Scroll surface and month slide

The month picker popover hangs from the header's bottom edge. `.month-picker-shell` is deliberately unpositioned, so the popover's containing block is the sticky `.mobile-header` and `top: 100%` lands on that header's bottom border on every viewport, whatever the safe-area inset makes the header's height. An offset from the picker button instead left it floating a few pixels below the header. Do not make the shell, the month switcher, or anything between them and the header positioned.

`.app-content` is the vertical overscroll surface for every route, continues behind the floating glass bottom navigation, and must retain enough bottom padding/scroll padding to expose the final content above that navigation. The top header remains opaque white, and route content enters over 0.65 s with the established blur-to-sharp motion. Changing the month with the header arrows replaces that motion with a two-layer horizontal slide on the shared `--page-slide-motion` timing, which reuses the navigation-detail push curve and which both layers must read from so they cannot drift apart: the outgoing month slides off one edge while the incoming month slides in from the other, matched 1:1 so the two read as one moving surface. Forward months enter from the right and earlier months from the left. The outgoing layer is a static DOM snapshot cloned when the arrow is pressed, so it must stay visually frozen — every animation and transition inside it is suppressed — and it is clipped to the content area rather than crossing the desktop rail. The snapshot's `scrollTop` is restored only after the layer joins the document, because a detached element ignores it. Scroll-driven values are copied onto the clone (`freezeScrollProgress`), and every `content-visibility: auto` element (the Budget category sections) gets the live element's current height, less its padding and borders because the intrinsic size is a content size, as its `contain-intrinsic-size` (`freezeIntrinsicSizes`): a fresh clone has no remembered sizes, so its off-screen categories fell back to the 170 px placeholder, the snapshot came out hundreds of pixels shorter than the page, and the restored scroll position was clamped, so the outgoing month visibly jumped when the arrow was pressed far down a long budget. Including the padding and borders left each copy a pixel too tall until it painted, and Chrome's scroll anchoring then moved the snapshot by the difference. A shown floating add button is copied too (`cloneFloatingAction`), positioned inside a full-size `.page-slide-out--floating` layer so it travels the page's width with the outgoing month instead of vanishing when the Budget view remounts. A month change with no matching snapshot, including the month picker and browser history, keeps the blur-to-sharp entrance instead of sliding half of it. Settings and Organize suppress the entrance animation entirely for month changes, however the month was chosen, and every tab change keeps the blur-to-sharp motion.

## Pull-to-refresh

### Native bounce

Every tab adds a touch pull-to-refresh at the top of `.app-content`, refetching the selected month's snapshot and announcing the outcome under that tab's own name. It does not fake the rubber band, and no script ever positions anything: `.app-content` keeps `overscroll-behavior-y: auto`, nothing calls `preventDefault`, and WebKit's own elastic bounce moves the content. Script-driven transforms read from `scrollTop` trail the compositor-driven bounce by a frame and visibly twitch, and a CSS scroll timeline cannot help because it clamps at zero and never sees overscroll. Content above the scroll origin is not painted in the overscroll gap either, because WebKit's scroll layer spans only the reachable scroll range, so the 58 px indicator band must live inside that range. `.app-content` therefore extends `--pull-band-height` up under the opaque header (negative top margin, matching top padding and `scroll-padding-top`), and the band is an absolutely positioned first child filling that padding. At rest the header covers it; the native bounce carries it out with the content, frame-exact, with no script involved. Anything sticky inside the scroller must add `--pull-band-height` to its `top` (the desktop `.right-rail` does), and the month-slide snapshot `.page-slide-out` repeats the same top padding so the outgoing page does not jump.

### Observation and thresholds

`pull-gesture.ts` only observes: from the first touch or scroll event until the finger is up and the bounce has returned, it reads `max(0, -scrollTop)` every frame and writes `--pull-progress`, which drives the dial's opacity, scale, and arc fill. Browsers that never report a negative `scrollTop` (Android, desktop) therefore have no pull-to-refresh, by decision. Because WebKit only rubber-bands a container that actually overflows, coarse-pointer view roots inside `.app-content` are at least `100% + 1px` tall. The pull never starts while a sheet, a pushed detail, or a list drag is active. The arc starts filling at 29 px of overscroll (`pullFillStart`, half of `--pull-band-height`, where the dial clears the header; keep the two in step) and completes at the 115 px release threshold, and release past it refreshes; a shorter release just bounces back.

### Refreshing without holding the content

The content is never held open while refreshing, because the web has no equivalent of UIRefreshControl's `contentInset`: WebKit's bounce always returns to zero, a negative `scrollTop` cannot be written, and every way tried of holding the content failed on device. Summing a CSS `translate` with the native bounce-back stutters however the curve is tuned. Swapping the bounce for a `translate` by zeroing `scrollTop` still jitters and flashes. Toggling the scroller's `overflow` makes WebKit rebuild the scroll layer, which flashes. Per-frame script compensation lags a frame and twitches. So on a refreshing release nothing touches the scroller and the content bounces back natively. Instead `markRelease` writes the release overscroll to `--pull-release` and enters the `releasing` state, which hides the in-scroller band and shows `.pull-refresh-float`, a 44 px white chip carrying the same dial, exactly where the band's dial was. The `refreshing` state then glides the chip on `--pull-hold-motion` to `--pull-float-top` below the header, where it floats over the content and spins. The chip's `z-index` keeps it above `.app-content` and below the header. At the end of a refresh the chip fades and shrinks on `--pull-settle-motion`; keep `settleDuration` in `pull-gesture.ts` equal to its duration, since it clears the state attribute once that transition ends. The spinner holds for at least 1,080 ms and at most 5 s, and the outcome is announced in a polite live region rather than a toast.

## Page entrance

Each tab and each launch enters with `page-enter` on the children of `.app-content--enter`: a 0.65 s fade up from 16 px below, un-blurring from 14 px and scaling from 0.99. Its `transform-origin` is `50% min(50%, var(--viewport-height) / 2)`, so the scale centers on the visible part of the page. With the default centered origin, a page's top edge also dropped by 0.5% of the page's full height, so a long Transactions list with production data slid up visibly farther than the short dev seed or a shorter tab.

## Landscape phones

### Island side

A phone on its side is at least 760 px wide, so it gets the desktop layout, adjusted by one block at the end of `responsive-motion.css` (`min-width: 760px`, `max-height: 500px`, landscape, `pointer: coarse`, so desktop windows and iPads never match). iOS reports the same left and right inset in landscape whichever side the Dynamic Island is on, so the inline head script `islandSideScript` (`src/app/safe-area-launch.ts`) marks `html` with `data-island` (`left` at a screen rotation of 90°, `right` at 270° or -90°, otherwise `top`) and updates it on every `resize` as well as on orientation changes, because waiting for the orientation event alone laid the page out for a left island first and then visibly jerked it over; `tokens.css` turns that into `--island-inset-left` and `--island-inset-right`: the real inset on the island's side and 0 on the other, defaulting to the left before the attribute exists. Landscape rules use these tokens, never `env(safe-area-inset-left/right)` directly.

### Layout

The sidebar column grows by `--island-inset-left` and pads its left edge by it, so the navigation clears the island when it is on the left, and the sidebar shows only the centered logo without the wordmark. The Budget page drops the right summary rail, centers its column at up to 520 px, and uses the mobile summary card (arc and Income, Planned, Spent row) instead of the desktop bars. The portrait text-size block at the top of `responsive-motion.css` (`max-width: 759px`) also matches this query, so category titles, item names, amounts, and summary labels keep their phone sizes, and the sidebar navigation labels grow to 15 px. Better Buddy docks 10 px from the right edge, or `--island-inset-right` plus 8 px when the island is on that side, and his left dock clears the widened sidebar. A 180° flip between the two landscape orientations fires no resize, so the attribute can still change after iOS has rotated the page; the sidebar column, its padding, the main panel's right padding, and Better Buddy's `left` and `right` therefore transition on `--island-shift-motion` (0.4 s on the push curve) and glide to the new side instead of snapping. When the island is on the right, `.app-main` pads its right edge by `--island-inset-right` instead, so the main panel, and the Budget column centered in it, keeps the same width and position relative to the sidebar in both landscape orientations.
